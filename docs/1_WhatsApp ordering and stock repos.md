# Saleor plus PyWa wins WhatsApp ordering

A solo Python and PostgreSQL developer should run **Saleor** as the stock and order system of record and write a small **PyWa plus FastAPI** bot on Meta's official WhatsApp Cloud API, with **Chatwoot** added later as an optional human inbox. No open source project in the notes delivers WhatsApp ordering with stock control out of the box, so every viable design glues a WhatsApp layer onto a stock system through its API. Saleor earns the first slot because it is the only Django and PostgreSQL commerce engine reviewed and because its stock behaviour on order is the best documented: stock is allocated when the checkout completes, and a short order returns an insufficient-stock error instead of an oversell. Its costs are real: a stack of five or more containers, roughly 4 GB of RAM, no purchase orders and no ready-made South African payment app. **InvenTree** is the lighter Python fallback when purchasing and supplier tracking matter more than checkout, and **Medusa** is the stronger pick only if the developer accepts TypeScript in exchange for existing Paystack, Yoco and WhatsApp notification plugins. WhatsApp running costs are small for inbound ordering, since each number receives 1,000 free service replies a month and later replies cost about $0.0095 in South Africa. Several load-bearing details were not verified against primary sources, including South African eligibility for Coexistence, Meta's own rand-market rate card and InvenTree's exact decrement step, and the final section lists them.

## No repository does the whole job, so the design is a stock system plus a bot

The research found no mature open source "WhatsApp store" on the official Cloud API. GitHub searches return many single-author order bots with 0 to 20 stars, useful as references and not as products ([GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=whatsapp+cloud+api+order+bot&type=repositories)). Searches for a WhatsApp integration for InvenTree returned nothing, and nothing relevant surfaced for Saleor ([InvenTree search](https://github.com/search?q=whatsapp+inventree&type=repositories), [Saleor search](https://github.com/search?q=whatsapp+saleor&type=repositories)). Medusa's community WhatsApp plugins either send outbound templates only or depend on Twilio's sandbox ([pkgstats](https://www.pkgstats.com/pkg:@sam-ael/medusa-plugin-whatsapp), [Medusa plugin listing](https://medusajs.com/plugins/medusa-plugin-whatsapp)). Odoo's own WhatsApp module is limited to the Enterprise edition, and the open OCA alternative is a beta shared inbox whose templates lack variables and buttons ([oec.sh guide](https://oec.sh/guides/odoo-whatsapp-integration), [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/odoo-addon-mail-gateway-whatsapp/)).

The one exception is ERPNext. The `frappe_whatsapp` app talks to the Cloud API directly, supports Flows and interactive messages, and has a companion chatbot app that can create documents from chat ([frappe_whatsapp README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/shridarpatil/frappe_whatsapp/master/README.md), [Frappe forum](https://discuss.frappe.io/t/whatsapp-integration-suite-for-frappe-messaging-chat-ui-chatbot/158000)). That path fails the database requirement, because a core contributor confirmed in June 2026 that PostgreSQL support exists only on develop and nightly builds, with no committed timeline ([Frappe forum](https://discuss.frappe.io/t/is-erpnext-officially-supported-with-postgresql/163329)). The app also had no push after 4 August 2026 at the time of research ([ecosyste.ms](https://repos.ecosyste.ms/api/v1/hosts/GitHub/repositories/shridarpatil%2Ffrappe_whatsapp)).

The decision therefore splits into two independent choices: which system owns products, stock and orders, and which code speaks to WhatsApp. Because the bot calls the stock system over HTTP, the absence of a native WhatsApp module stops being a deciding factor, and stack fit, stock semantics and hosting footprint decide instead.

## Saleor leads the stock systems on stack fit and verified stock behaviour

### Commerce engines: Saleor, Medusa and Vendure

All three engines were alive in November 2026. Saleor's repository page shows 23.4k stars under BSD-3-Clause, a tracker recorded version 3.23.45 on 3 November 2026, and an upgrade guide to 3.24 was last updated on 19 October 2026 ([GitHub](https://github.com/saleor/saleor/), [SelfHost Hub](https://selfhost-hub.pages.dev/en/saleor), [Saleor docs](https://docs.saleor.io/upgrade-guides/core/3-23-to-3-24)). Medusa shows 36.5k stars with an MIT core and published v2.22.7 on 13 November 2026, the tenth release since 22 September ([GitHub](https://github.com/medusajs/medusa), [releasealert.dev](https://releasealert.dev/github/medusajs/medusa)). Vendure sat at v3.8.1 and has been GPLv3 since version 3.0, a point that several 2026 comparison articles still report wrongly as MIT ([SelfHost Hub](https://selfhost-hub.pages.dev/en/vendure), [Vendure announcement](https://vendure.io/blog/license-change-announcement)).

Saleor is the only one of the three written in Python, on Django, Celery and PostgreSQL with a GraphQL-only API ([Contra Collective](https://contracollective.com/blog/saleor-vs-medusa-vs-vendure-open-source-headless-commerce-2026), [GitHub](https://github.com/saleor/saleor/)). Its stock model answers the core requirement precisely. Each variant holds a quantity, an allocated quantity and a reserved quantity per warehouse ([Saleor docs](https://docs.saleor.io/developer/stock/overview)). Completion of a checkout allocates stock for tracked lines, and a shortfall raises an insufficient-stock error ([Saleor docs](https://docs.saleor.io/developer/stock/stock-allocation)). Optional timed reservations protect a cart during checkout, and without them the first checkout to complete wins ([Saleor docs](https://docs.saleor.io/developer/stock/stock-reservation)). A guest order needs only a short mutation sequence: `checkoutCreate`, email and address updates, a delivery method, then `checkoutComplete`, which returns the order ([Saleor docs](https://docs.saleor.io/developer/checkout/api-guide)). The bot therefore needs no custom decrement logic.

Saleor's weaknesses are operational and functional. A 2026 self-hosting guide lists an API container, a Celery worker, a dashboard, PostgreSQL and Valkey, asks for 4 GB of free RAM and calls the result overkill for small stores ([selfhosting-sh](https://selfhosting-sh.pages.dev/apps/saleor/)). The stock docs mention neither purchase orders nor a configurable low-stock threshold, and alerts arrive only as webhooks such as `PRODUCT_VARIANT_OUT_OF_STOCK` and `PRODUCT_VARIANT_STOCK_UPDATED` ([Saleor docs](https://docs.saleor.io/developer/stock/overview)). A targeted search found no Saleor payment app for Paystack, PayFast or Yoco.

Medusa is the lighter engine, at an estimated 2 GB minimum across three containers, and it reserves stock per location when an order is placed ([selfhosting-sh comparison](https://selfhosting-sh.pages.dev/compare/medusa-vs-saleor/), [Medusa docs](https://docs.medusajs.com/resources/commerce-modules/inventory/concepts/index.html.md)). It also has a Paystack provider for v2 and a Yoco provider, although the Yoco package had not been published for about ten months ([GitHub](https://www.github.com/a11rew/medusa-payment-paystack), [pkgstats](https://www.pkgstats.com/pkg:medusa-payment-yoco)). The price is TypeScript, no official Docker images, and minor releases that regularly break things ([selfhosting.sh](https://selfhosting.sh/apps/medusa/), [Medusa releases](https://github.com/medusajs/medusa/releases)). Vendure has the smallest dependency surface, but it allocates stock only when an order reaches a payment-authorised or payment-settled state by default, which suits EFT and cash-on-delivery sellers poorly without customisation ([Vendure docs](https://docs.vendure.io/current/core/core-concepts/stock-control.md)).

### Inventory and ERP systems: InvenTree, Odoo Community, ERPNext and Dolibarr

InvenTree is the cleanest stack match among the pure stock systems: MIT, Python and Django with Django REST Framework, PostgreSQL support, about 7.9k stars and a push on 14 November 2026 ([ecosyste.ms](https://repos.ecosyste.ms/api/v1/hosts/GitHub/repositories/inventree%2FInvenTree), [InvenTree README](https://git.invenhost.com/inventree/InvenTree/raw/commit/93b44ad8e6abdc970969360708aed3ff8189ec93/README.md)). Docker Hub listed tag 1.7.2 on 12 November 2026, and an official Python client sits on PyPI at 0.25.1 ([Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/v2/repositories/inventree/inventree/tags?page_size=8), [PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/inventree/json)). It models sales orders, allocations and shipments, and release 1.4.0 added sales order auto-allocation and transfer orders ([InvenTree docs](https://git.invenhost.com/inventree/InvenTree/raw/commit/b568e82d3177eb33be0f14f549c3ba5b39daad58/docs/docs/sales/sales_order.md), [InvenTree blog](https://inventree.org/blog/2026/06/16/1.4.0)). Two things hold it back. Its design centres on parts, bills of materials and builds and it has no storefront, so checkout, pricing rules and invoicing fall to custom code. The same 1.4.0 release also changed API methods and status codes, so a solo developer must pin versions.

Odoo Community is Python on PostgreSQL with the largest community at about 55.7k stars, and version 20 arrived in September 2026 ([ecosyste.ms](https://repos.ecosyste.ms/api/v1/hosts/GitHub/repositories/odoo%2Fodoo), [ecosire](https://ecosire.com/blog/whats-new-in-odoo-20)). It offers the most complete sales, purchase and stock story in one system, yet it brings its own ORM and module framework, manual major upgrades, and Enterprise gating of official WhatsApp and advanced barcode features ([ecosire](https://ecosire.com/blog/odoo-community-vs-enterprise)). New integrations must also target the JSON-2 API, because the legacy RPC services are scheduled for removal by Odoo 22 ([Cybrosys](https://www.cybrosys.com/blog/how-to-use-the-odoo-20-external-api-with-json-2-xml-rpc-deprecation-and-api-keys)). Dolibarr is active under GPL-3.0, supports PostgreSQL and covers stock, orders and purchasing, but it is PHP ([ecosyste.ms](https://repos.ecosyste.ms/api/v1/hosts/GitHub/repositories/Dolibarr%2Fdolibarr), [Dolibarr wiki](https://wiki.dolibarr.org/index.php/Releases)).

| Candidate | Licence | State in November 2026 | Stack and database | Stock on order | WhatsApp path | Fit for this seller |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Saleor | BSD-3-Clause | 3.23.45, 3.24 guide published | Python, Django, PostgreSQL, GraphQL | Allocated at checkout completion, oversell blocked | Custom bot on checkout API | Best overall, heaviest footprint |
| Medusa | MIT core | v2.22.7, near-weekly releases | TypeScript, PostgreSQL, Redis, REST | Reservation at order placement | Custom bot, outbound plugin exists | Best if TypeScript is acceptable |
| Vendure | GPLv3 | v3.8.1 | TypeScript, NestJS, GraphQL, PostgreSQL or others | Allocated at payment state | Custom bot | Stable, weaker payment-state fit |
| InvenTree | MIT | 1.7.2 image, fast cadence | Python, Django, PostgreSQL, REST | Allocation to order and shipment | Custom bot, no plugin found | Best lightweight Python fallback |
| Odoo Community | LGPL per vendor source | 20.0 nightlies | Python, own framework, PostgreSQL | Not verified | OCA inbox (beta) or custom bot | Capable, heavier learning curve |
| ERPNext | GPL-3.0 | v16.55.0 | Python, Frappe, MariaDB in practice | Optional reservation feature | frappe_whatsapp suite | Fails PostgreSQL requirement |
| Dolibarr | GPL-3.0 | 24.0.2 | PHP, PostgreSQL supported | Not verified | None found | Fails Python preference |

## PyWa on the official Cloud API is the WhatsApp layer, and Chatwoot is optional

PyWa is the clear library choice. It is MIT licensed, released version 4.5.0 on 24 September 2026, supports sync and async use, and plugs into an existing FastAPI app ([GitHub](https://github.com/david-lev/pywa), [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pywa/)). Its client exposes catalog and product sends and its types include `Order` and `Product`, and its CLI downloads an official order-bot example ([pywa/client.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/david-lev/pywa/master/pywa/client.py), [PyWa CLI docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/david-lev/pywa/master/docs/source/content/cli.rst)). Two cautions apply. The listener feature stops working with multiple workers, so conversation state belongs in PostgreSQL or in a single-worker process. The project is also effectively run by one maintainer who now advertises a hosted product, a bus-factor risk that the MIT licence softens. The older heyoo library last shipped in March 2024, and its active fork is AGPL-3.0 ([PyPI heyoo](https://pypi.org/project/heyoo/), [GitHub](https://github.com/filipporomani/whatsapp-python)).

The flow builders lose on licence or maintenance. Typebot's licence file is the Functional Source License, and its WhatsApp channel shows only three buttons at a time and skips the payment block ([Typebot LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/baptisteArno/typebot.io/main/LICENSE), [Typebot docs](https://docs.typebot.com/deploy/whatsapp/overview.md)). n8n uses the Sustainable Use License, Flowise was archived in August 2026, and Rasa Open Source sits in maintenance mode ([n8n licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n8n-io/n8n/master/LICENSE.md), [Flowise sunset](https://flowiseai.com/sunset), [Rasa README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RasaHQ/rasa/main/README.md)). Internal use of Typebot or n8n is permitted, so they remain usable, but a code-first bot gives a Python developer more control over list messages, Flows and product messages.

Chatwoot is the strongest shared inbox for the human side of product questions: MIT core, about 39.9k stars, a push on 13 November 2026, and a production stack of Rails, Sidekiq, PostgreSQL 16 and Redis ([GitHub](https://github.com/chatwoot/chatwoot), [compose file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chatwoot/chatwoot/develop/docker-compose.production.yaml)). It does not capture structured orders. Its Cloud provider code sends text, simple interactive messages, attachments and templates, with no catalog or product sending found ([provider source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chatwoot/chatwoot/develop/app/services/whatsapp/providers/whatsapp_cloud_service.rb)). Chatwoot is therefore a phase-three addition for handoff, not the ordering engine, and a single seller can skip it at first. Whatomate is a lighter single-binary alternative on Go and PostgreSQL under AGPL-3.0 ([Whatomate README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/shridarpatil/whatomate/main/README.md)).

Unofficial libraries such as Baileys, WAHA and Evolution API in its default mode imitate WhatsApp Web. Secondary sources describe them as breaches of WhatsApp's terms that carry suspension and permanent-ban risk for the number ([Zylos research](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-01-26-whatsapp-api-automation)). The seller's number is the shopfront, so the recommendation excludes them.

## Platform rules, costs, payments and law shape the bot's behaviour

**Messaging rules.** Free-form and interactive replies are allowed only within 24 hours of the customer's last message, and templates are the only way to write outside that window ([Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing), [360dialog](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/messaging/message-types/interactive-messages)). Reply-button messages hold three buttons and lists hold ten options. The Cloud API also supports single-product, multi-product and catalog messages plus a customer cart that arrives as an order webhook ([Meta catalogs](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/catalogs/catalogs-overview/)). A new business portfolio can send templates to 250 unique recipients per rolling 24 hours, and replies inside customer-initiated windows do not count toward that cap ([AiSensy](https://m.aisensy.com/blog/whatsapp-message-limits-guide/)). An inbound ordering bot can therefore launch before business verification completes. Meta's terms bar general-purpose AI assistants from the platform since 15 January 2026 but allow AI that serves a business's own customers, so an LLM-assisted bot must stay scoped to the seller's products and orders ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/18/whatssapp-changes-its-terms-to-bar-general-purpose-chatbots-from-its-platform)).

**Message costs.** Per-message pricing replaced conversation pricing on 1 July 2025, and from 1 October 2026 Meta also bills delivered service messages at the market's utility rate ([Meta pricing updates](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/non-template-messages/)). Each number receives 1,000 free service messages a month, and replies stop after that if no payment method is on file ([Tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/10/01/whatsapp-business-platform-charges-start-today-but-the-first-1000-replies-are-free/)). Third-party pages report South African rates of **$0.0379 for marketing and $0.0095 for utility, authentication and service messages**, roughly R0.69 and R0.17 ([Whautomate](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-south-africa)). As a worked estimate, 300 orders a month at ten bot replies each produce 3,000 service messages, 2,000 of them billable, for about $19 or R350. A seller under 100 orders at that depth pays nothing. Marketing templates cost about four times a service reply, so broadcasts, not order handling, drive the bill. Meta's own Business Agent costs $2.00 per million tokens, about 4 to 5 US cents per message, which makes a self-built bot the cheaper route ([Meta pricing updates](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/non-template-messages/)).

**Business app and Coexistence.** Coexistence lets one number run in the free Business app and on the API, with app messages free and API messages billed, but onboarding must go through a Solution Partner or Tech Provider, and Meta lists the app's catalog, orders, labels and quick replies as unsupported in that mode ([Meta onboarding](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users)). A dedicated number registered directly on the Cloud API avoids that dependency and suits a developer-built bot better.

**Payments.** Native in-chat payments are reported only for India and Brazil, so South African sellers send a gateway link ([Infobip](https://www.infobip.com/blog/whatsapp-payments)). Two secondary sources state that Stripe does not serve South African entities ([CheckoutJoy](https://checkoutjoy.com/docs/guides/south-africa/), [ZATech wiki](https://zatech-wiki.pages.dev/blog/payment-providers-in-south-africa)). An aggregator verified on 4 November 2026 lists Paystack at 2.9% plus R1 for local cards and 2% for EFT, PayFast at 3.2% plus R2 for cards, Peach at 2.95% plus R1.50, and Ozow at 1.5% for Pay by Bank, mostly excluding VAT ([Mctaba](https://mctaba.com/learn/payments/south-africa/gateways)). Yoco publishes a dedicated payment link product ([Yoco](https://www.yoco.com/za/payment-link/)). Fixed fees matter on small baskets: a R100 card order costs about R5.20 at PayFast against about R3.90 at Paystack.

**Legal basics.** A law firm's summaries of the statutes give the working rules, and they are not legal advice. POPIA section 69 requires opt-in consent for electronic direct marketing unless the existing-customer exception applies, and that exception needs an opt-out at collection and in every message ([MJK Inc](https://mjkinc.co.za/popia/electronic-marketing-section-69)). The ECT Act requires disclosure of the seller's identity, address, full price including delivery, payment method and returns policy, and it requires that the customer can review and correct the order before commitment ([MJK Inc](https://mjkinc.co.za/compliance/ecommerce-ecta)). It also grants a seven-day cooling-off right from receipt, with exclusions such as foodstuffs and personalised goods. In practice the bot must show a full order summary with the total and ask for explicit confirmation before it sends a payment link, and it must record marketing opt-in separately from the order.

## One 4 GB server and a five-phase build

The recommended architecture has four parts. Saleor (API, worker, dashboard, PostgreSQL, Valkey) holds products, prices, stock and orders. A FastAPI service with PyWa receives the Cloud API webhook, verifies signatures, de-duplicates message ids, keeps conversation state in PostgreSQL and calls Saleor's GraphQL API with an app token. A payment gateway (Paystack or Yoco as first candidates) issues links and posts webhooks. Chatwoot joins later for human handoff. The stock system stays the single source of truth, a rule that matters because WhatsApp's catalog carries only an availability flag and not stock counts ([Meta catalogs](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/catalogs/catalogs-overview/)). List messages driven directly from Saleor avoid a catalog sync job, so native carts stay optional. A zero-star reference repository in the same stack documents the right production concerns: signature checks, idempotent webhooks, prices read from the system of record and human handoff ([xDIVx/whatsapp-order-bot](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xDIVx/whatsapp-order-bot/main/README.md)).

| Phase | Scope | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Foundations | Dedicated number, Meta business portfolio, payment method on file, start business verification, gateway account, ECT Act and POPIA pages | Compliant base, no code |
| 1. Stock system | Deploy Saleor 3.23 with Docker on a 4 GB server, load products and stock for one warehouse and one channel, enable track inventory, answer WhatsApp by hand | Working stock management |
| 2. Ordering bot | FastAPI plus PyWa: product search and stock answers from Saleor, list and button flows, order summary and confirmation, checkout mutations, payment link, gateway webhook marks the order paid | Orders decrement stock automatically |
| 3. Operations | Low-stock WhatsApp alert from Saleor stock webhooks, utility templates for order status, Chatwoot handoff, optional scoped LLM for product questions | Less manual work |
| 4. Optional | Meta catalog sync and native carts, Flows for address capture, purchasing in InvenTree or a spreadsheet if supplier tracking becomes necessary | Richer in-chat experience |

Two decision points deserve attention. If 4 GB of RAM or Celery operations prove too heavy, InvenTree plus the same bot is the fallback, with a custom availability rule of on-hand minus allocated and prices kept by the bot service. If the developer would rather maintain TypeScript than write a payment integration, Medusa with its Paystack provider is the faster route to paid orders.

## What was not verified

The notes mark many points as unconfirmed, and the following ones affect the recommendation directly. **Saleor:** the 3.24 release date and status, minimum Python and PostgreSQL versions, whether reservations are on by default, the draft order flow for staff-entered orders, and the channel setting for unpaid orders were not confirmed from documentation. Vendor-published hardware minimums were not found for any engine, so all RAM figures come from third-party guides. **Payments:** no PayFast integration was confirmed for any engine, Stripe's country list was not fetched, payment links were confirmed from a primary page only for Yoco, and gateway fees come from one aggregator. **InvenTree:** the step at which on-hand stock drops, whether allocated stock is excluded from availability, low-stock notifications, barcode support and minimum hardware were not confirmed, because the documentation site was blocked. **Odoo:** the standard sale-to-delivery stock flow, the licence file text and Community's inclusion of the website shop were not confirmed from primary sources, and vendor blogs disagree on the last point. **WhatsApp:** Meta's own rate card for South Africa and its messaging limits page were blocked, so rates and tiers rest on third-party pages. Sources conflict on whether the free entry point window lasts 72 hours or up to 7 days. South African eligibility for Coexistence and for Meta Business Agent, Flow publishing prerequisites, and the order webhook payload were not confirmed. The single-webhook-per-number constraint that governs how Chatwoot and a custom bot share a number was not checked against Meta's documentation. **Chatwoot:** its handling of an incoming cart order was not confirmed. **PyWa:** the contents of the order-bot example were not read. **General:** South African hosting prices, VAT and courier localisation, document requirements for business verification, and the Consumer Protection Act's warranty sections were not researched. No account was found of anyone running these stock systems behind a Cloud API ordering bot in production, and no code was executed during this research.

## Conclusion

The market gap is itself the main finding. In November 2026 the open source world offers excellent stock engines and one excellent Python WhatsApp library, but the join between them remains a few hundred lines that each seller writes alone. That favours a developer who already works in Python, because the join is where Saleor's GraphQL checkout, PyWa's typed handlers and a gateway webhook meet, and it is small enough to own outright.

The October 2026 pricing change also shifts design priorities. Every outbound bot turn now counts against a 1,000-message allowance, so terse flows that reach a confirmed order in few messages are cheaper as well as kinder to customers. The first week of phase two should therefore test the unverified items that carry the most risk, namely unpaid order handling in Saleor and the chosen gateway's link and webhook behaviour, before any conversational polish.
